## Previous scope and the contradiction

Proposition 3.1 in the inspected arXiv text proves that sufficiently large positive t yields simple real roots and interlacing. The counterexample leaves that result intact. What fails is the conjectured single threshold on the full positive axis: a real-rooted cubic at t=1/3 is followed by a nonreal pair at t=1/2.

All coefficients alpha_n are one; the recurrence is monic with strictly positive Favard coefficients, and its infinite compactly supported positive probability measure is established by the written Jacobi-operator argument. The example does not depend on the degree-one oversight, a zero coefficient, a finite polynomial list, or isolated repeated roots. It does not refute separate Hermite, Charlier, or Lommel assertions with their chosen normalizations.

Durán's May 2025 papers address related linear combinations. The general paper permits a normalization adapted to a positive measure and takes sufficiently large degree for fixed-length combinations; the Hermite paper examines specific normalizations and coefficient-polynomial conditions. Neither result supplies the universal fixed-degree threshold contradicted here.
